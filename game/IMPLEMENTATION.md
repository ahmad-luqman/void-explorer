## Coordinate and travel contract

Canonical addresses contain three integer cells of 1,000,000 km each and three double-precision offsets normalized to [-500,000, 500,000) km. Large displacements are split before adding local offsets. Differences subtract cells before adding offsets; saves never depend on the renderer's current origin. Supported cells are bounded to ±1 billion per axis. Crossing that limit stops engines and navigation at the last valid address with a visible survey-range message.

The same 1,024 deterministic systems and 3,072 planets now span roughly 15 light-years: non-home system spacing is multiplied by 100 million, while the home system, local planetary layouts, seeds, radii and terrain retain their existing scale. The simulation rebases its working origin beyond 2 million km during travel, rebuilding working body positions from their addresses. Attached surface locations and contact/scenery geometry use native planet coordinates; stale contact work is invalidated when the origin changes. Planetary meshes remain reusable. Distant star points draw on a camera-relative shell using the real destination directions, so the camera far plane does not hide reachable systems. System charts subtract the inspected system's cells before plotting local bodies; galaxy charts and distance labels support light-years.

Pulse travel accelerates with deep-space clearance up to 5 trillion km/s, retaining existing local flight controls and near-body braking. Collision substeps choose the nearest system at each sample and conservatively bound travel outside a 50,000 km sphere enclosing its bodies, then use the established fine checks nearby. This makes interstellar journeys practical without skipping a system in a single unchecked step. It is game travel, not relativistic physics or orbital mechanics.

Version-4 saves store the canonical address and the existing rotation clock/native attached poses. Restore checks address bounds and agreement with attached poses before mutating live state. Version-2 attached saves migrate through the old stationary planet frame; version-3 attached saves already have native coordinates. Legacy flight saves preserve their offset from the nearest system in the old compressed universe and relocate to that system in the expanded universe. Flights resume stopped. Rotation still pauses while saved ground is restored and does not advance offline.

## Cross-session terrain contract

Both terrain workers share a device-local IndexedDB cache with transactional least-recently-used eviction at 48 entries or 32 MiB of typed-array buffers. Database bookkeeping has additional browser-managed overhead. Complete native planetary meshes and precise contact meshes are stored; independent per-tile refinement is a future architecture change. The planetary worker checks its existing 12-entry/12-MiB memory cache first, then disk, then seeded generation. Disk signatures include the geometry revision and world/quality inputs. Planet views retain the existing angular/altitude compatibility rules; contact views reuse a native anchor only within 400 m. Copies are retained before transferable buffers are posted to the renderer.

Stored geometry is validated for typed buffers, indices, dimensions, finite/bounded values, native axes, body identity, and byte accounting before installation. Invalid payloads are discarded and corrupt metadata clears only this disposable cache. Denied access, quota failures, unavailable storage, and 1.5-second open/transaction timeouts disable disk for that worker session while generation continues. Serial worker queues prevent overlapping requests from corrupting retained transition state; IndexedDB transactions coordinate eviction across workers. Counters distinguish generated, memory and disk responses and report cache hits, writes, errors, entries and bytes.

The deterministic generator remains authoritative. This is an optional local performance cache, not expedition save storage or cross-device synchronization. Browsers may evict it, and no persistence permission is required. No server database or object store is used.

## Architecture

- `lib/flight/biomes.ts`: seeded native-frame climate/height regions, surface palettes and vegetation density.

- `lib/flight/coordinates.ts`: canonical integer-cell addresses, normalization, precise relative differences, validation, and light-year units.
- `lib/flight/simulation.ts`: renderer-independent position, orientation, throttle, proximity speed limits, obstacle-aware autopilot, and collision substeps.
- `lib/flight/renderer.ts`: camera-relative Three.js rendering, persistent planetary meshes, atmospheric rim and sky, rings, binary suns, star points, ship, dust streaks, and bloom.
- `lib/flight/renderer-factory.ts` and `gpu/`: optional asynchronous WebGPU setup, TSL effect/material conversion, native bloom, compatibility fallback, and cancellation cleanup.
- `lib/flight/environment.ts`: shared altitude envelope, sun directions, daylight/twilight, planet-specific sky colors, haze, and star visibility.
- `lib/flight/water-material.ts`: planet-relative animated water normals and roughness, composed with globe/patch masks and ground material shaders. No geometry displacement or extra water mesh.
- `lib/flight/planet-terrain.ts` and `terrain.worker.ts`: bounded adaptive cube-sphere generation using the shared elevation function. Indexed transferable buffers replace the full planet atomically; shared edge vertices join different refinement levels and cube faces. `terrain.ts` retains the common biome palette.
- `lib/flight/terrain-cache.ts`: worker-owned LRU reuse of complete planetary meshes, bounded by both entry count and buffer bytes.
- `lib/flight/terrain-storage.ts` and `terrain-validation.ts`: bounded native IndexedDB mesh storage shared across workers, revisioned signatures, payload checks, and fail-open generation.
- `lib/flight/terrain-transition.ts`: worker-side radial projection through a triangle BVH, plus shared shape/color blending and contact protection.
- `lib/flight/terrain-stream.ts`: movement/altitude/quality refresh thresholds and bounded look-ahead requests for the precise contact surface.
- `lib/flight/contact.ts` and `contact.worker.ts`: graded indexed terrain generated in a worker: an 18.75 m grid through the central 2.4 km, expanding to cells up to 2 km across toward the horizon. A 48 km visible radius replaces the old 1.05 km contact disk. Foot and landing-gear queries raycast the exact Float32 triangles rendered on screen, including neighboring grid cells at rounded edges.
- `lib/flight/terrain-seam.ts`: a buried outer skirt joins detailed terrain to the coarse surface; front-side cylindrical masks keep the opposite hemisphere intact.
- `lib/flight/surface-material.ts`: native planet-anchored procedural gravel and mineral variation, with lower water roughness. These are material details, not new collision geometry.
- `lib/flight/surface.ts`: landing, parked craft, walking, boarding, and vertical takeoff states. Landing rejects water, slopes above 12 degrees, and uneven gear contact. Walking rejects water and slopes above 35 degrees.
- `lib/flight/persistence.ts`: validated versioned local saves, automatic stable-phase saves, explicit save, and ground-ready resume. Flight resumes stopped.
- `lib/flight/ship.ts`: asynchronous loading of the self-contained AURORA GLB, with a retained procedural fallback in `ship-fallback.ts`. The authored asset contains four separate wings, ivory armor, teal glass, twin controllable engine cores, and three landing pads. Late loads after renderer disposal release their resources. The physical ship uses a consistent meter scale; the title screen retains an illustrative pose.
- `public/models/aurora-v1.glb`: Blender-authored export, 12 meshes / 3,152 triangles / seven materials / 181,956 bytes. Editable source, rebuild script, report, and studio render live in the root `models/aurora/` directory.
- `components/star-chart.tsx`: interactive SVG maps of real X/Z world coordinates, system inspection, planet/star previews, zoom/pan, nearest-first search, discovered markers, and direct-bearing lines. A keyboard-accessible list complements the map.
- `lib/flight/navigation.ts`: terrain-aware range, closing speed, approach-distance ETA, and navigation guidance without changing simulation motion.
- `app/page.tsx`: start screen, flight instruments, controls, pause, settings, navigation dialog, and optional engine audio.

## Terrain transitions and reuse

The terrain worker retains up to 12 complete planetary meshes and 12 MiB of typed-array buffers, evicting the least recently used mesh when either bound would be exceeded. Cache signatures include the world identity/seed/radius/type, quality, pixel target, projection budget, and leaf cap. A view is reusable only within the existing altitude/angular refresh thresholds; a hit keeps its original observer anchor so repeated hits cannot drift beyond coverage. The projection budget uses the narrowest supported field of view (58°), avoiding cache fragmentation while the flight camera animates. Viewport budget changes request matching terrain. Cached buffers are copied before transfer, so posting a response cannot detach reusable storage.

For replacement of an existing cached mesh, a worker-side triangle BVH projects the new vertices radially onto the previous mesh and interpolates its colors. The renderer blends these projected positions/colors into the final mesh over 0.8 seconds of clamped frame time with smooth endpoint easing. Shared target indices stay unchanged through the blend, and a radial bounding sphere encloses both endpoints. The main thread updates bounded vertex arrays; it does not perform triangle searches. New requests for the same planet wait until the blend finishes. Old-system replies no longer clear a newer request's pending flag.

Vertices within 68 km of the observer use final geometry immediately, tapering to full morphing by 136 km. This protects the 48 km contact disk and its buried seam while the surrounding planet changes detail. The precise contact mesh, landing heights, walking coordinates, and collision queries are not morphed. Colors can continue blending on the surrounding mesh. The initial fallback globe and replacements whose prior mesh was evicted still switch directly. Reprojection onto different triangulations reduces popping but does not reproduce every previous triangle exactly, especially when coarsening. The memory cache is session-only and now sits above the bounded cross-session disk cache described above. Both store whole meshes; independent tile reuse remains future work.

Five added unit cases cover radial projection and color bounds, exact blend endpoints, outward triangles during refinement/coarsening, local contact protection, compatible-view reuse with source-buffer ownership, and both LRU limits. The new browser journey revisits views, verifies cache hits without another generation miss, waits for each quality transition to finish, and checks stationary walking positions throughout, allowing only 10 micrometers of double-precision roundoff after resuming ground projection. It also checks the cache bounds and browser shader errors. The verified on-foot view is preserved as `art/milestones/terrain-transition-ground.png`. Transition preparation time and total transferred bytes (including morph endpoints) are reported separately from cache lookup/generation time. The legacy `generated` counter counts installed responses, including hits; `cache.misses` tracks actual generated meshes. In the recorded revisit test, 7 installed responses comprised 5 misses and 2 hits, retaining 1,437,936 bytes in 5 entries. The final hit took about 0.1 ms to look up and about 25 ms to prepare its transition on the worker. These are one-run software-rendered development measurements, not hardware frame-rate claims.

## WebGPU rendering contract

Automatic mode requests a WebGPU adapter/device in a secure browser context, then initializes Three's WebGPURenderer. Missing adapters and initialization failures fall back to the original WebGL renderer; a failed GPU context gets a new canvas. Cancelling an obsolete React initialization releases its device without replacing a newer canvas. The renderer label reports the actual backend. Settings stores Automatic/WebGL locally, with a URL override that also works if storage is blocked. Switching an active expedition requires a successful stable-phase save; device loss stops simulation and engine sound, attempts a stable save, and offers a WebGL recovery.

Both backends consume the same scene, simulation, worker terrain, authored GLB, uniforms, and quality settings. WebGPU replaces custom GLSL effects with TSL node materials for nebula/sky, stellar halos, atmospheric rims, rings, and clouds. The `scripts/port-shaders.mjs` generator derives those functions from the retained GLSL sources; run `node scripts/port-shaders.mjs` after shader edits. The conversion preserves live RGB Color uniforms, inlines functions that capture uniforms, and avoids undefined negative-base exponentiation. TSL terrain materials reproduce clipping, contact coverage, mineral color, and vertex-interpolated water masks and animated normals. High uses a native scene/bloom pipeline; Low renders directly. Cached shadow graphs stay allocated while intensity/update flags disable distant and Low-quality shadows, avoiding invalid cached render passes after switching quality. A new shadow target receives an initial render before inactive shadows can sample it, preventing stale texture bindings when shadows become active near the ground.

The WebGPU backend is loaded dynamically; browsers falling back before GPU detection use the existing WebGL bundle. No dependencies were added or upgraded. Three's lighting/color pipeline can produce visible differences between backends; this port does not promise pixel-identical images, volumetric effects, or improved frame rate on every device. Hardware GPU profiling, Safari/Firefox coverage, and independent per-tile refinement remain future validation/work.

## Adaptive planet and streaming contract

The nearest planet refines on a worker below 5,000 km altitude. Six cube faces start with coarse coverage; a priority queue splits tiles whose sampled center/edge deviation projects largest at the observer. Refinement reaches depth 9, with hard limits of 3,000 leaves on High and 1,500 on Low. The 2/5 pixel thresholds are targets, not guarantees: the geometry budget takes priority. The back hemisphere retains coarse coverage. Screen height sets projection scale at each request; there is no directional frustum culling or hardware timing feedback yet.

Cube-space edge registries insert neighboring fine corners into coarse tile fans and reuse their indices across face boundaries. Unit checks require every edge to belong to exactly two triangles and the closed sphere's Euler characteristic to be 2. All vertices use the existing analytic terrain/sea level, so destinations, landing coordinates, and saves are unchanged. The old fixed 160×160 angular flight patch is removed. A startup globe covers the planet until the first result arrives, then its resources are released; each later mesh remains visible until its replacement arrives. Contact coverage retains the precise 18.75 m central cells and buried boundary skirt, with a 10 m overlap against the adaptive planet.

Planet requests are limited to one outstanding job and at most one every 500 ms. Separate approach/retreat ratios and angular thresholds avoid rebuilding for tiny movements; quality changes request the corresponding budget. Results from old systems are discarded, and failed workers wait one second before retrying. Replacement meshes blend using the transition contract above; distant faceting and residual detail changes can still be visible. This is a bounded full-planet rebuild per request, not persistent individual-tile caching.

Contact requests predict 0.35 seconds of tangential travel, capped at 350 m ahead so the current observer stays inside the dense core even after a sharp turn. Replacement begins at 550 m of lateral movement or an 800 m predicted offset. Existing rendered/collision ground remains active while the worker runs, and low-flight protection still stops at the dense boundary if data is delayed. Landing and landed phases retain their fixed contact patch.

## Lighting and water contract

Primary and companion light directions come from system body positions. Their direct contribution fades below the pilot's local horizon during atmospheric flight; low ambient light preserves silhouettes at night. Ocean, desert, and ice worlds have distinct zenith, horizon, and twilight palettes. Sky, fog, and star visibility share a smooth 0–160 km altitude envelope. Planetary rim brightness follows both sun directions. These are stylized approximations; there is no physical scattering solver, eclipse simulation, or global terrain shadowing.

High graphics uses a single 1,024² PCF ship shadow map over a 140 m region centered on the craft. It activates near the ground or while walking when the key sun is above the horizon. Ship meshes and instanced rock/mineral fields cast shadows; the ship, props, and detailed terrain receive them within this local map. Low graphics omits this pass and bloom. Custom sky/rim/ring shaders use the same tone-mapping and output-color conversion as built-in materials, so switching to direct rendering preserves the palette. The shadow target follows the same camera-relative origin as the ship; its render target is released on disposal.

Ocean shaders share planet-relative wave phases and time across globe, flight patch, and contact mesh. Water gets a smooth radial normal, subtle animated normal variation within 12 km, and reduced roughness for solar glints. The geometry, coast boundary, water landing guards, and saved contact positions are unchanged. Waves are shading only; reflections of terrain/ships and displaced wave geometry remain future work. Signed seabed elevations now drive teal shallows, darker offshore color and filtered shoreward foam bands. The depth attribute travels through workers, bounded caches and adaptive mesh morphs; disposable cache revision 7 rejects older buffers. Contact-protected morph regions hold depth and color with their geometry. The WebGPU material applies vertex colors before replacing the wet surface color, matching the WebGL order. Manual flight now uses the envelope and coverage guards described below. The earlier 5 km description was incomplete: the previous collision code used a 3 m center-only floor wherever a contact patch was available, and 5 km otherwise.

## Clouds and scenery contract

`clouds.ts` builds one 128×64 weather shell per planet, with vertices 18 km above the shared terrain heightfield (16,128 triangles). The same shell is visible above and below it. Seeded noise drifts slowly in planet coordinates; sun directions and palette determine its tint. Fog, output-color conversion, and a near-camera opacity fade avoid a hard opaque crossing. Low graphics omits the finest noise octave. These are thin transparent layers, not volumetric clouds, and they cast no ground shadows.

`scenery.ts` places rock/mineral candidates on fixed planet cube-face cells. Identity, size, yaw, and tint remain stable when the observer moves. ## Manual low-flight contract

`flight-clearance.ts` uses a rotation-independent 18 m ship envelope plus a 2 m buffer. It samples the exact rendered ground triangles at the center and eight points around the footprint, using each triangle normal to protect wings and the nose over slopes. Rock/mineral collisions use conservative capsules along each prop's tilted normal, enclosing the actual translated meshes while allowing flight above them. This is deliberately more conservative than exact hull contact; landing continues to use the separate gear-contact procedure.

Below 2 km, collision and approach-speed limits use this ground/obstacle clearance. Travel steps shrink from the bounded deep-space distances described above, through at most 20 km near a system, to 5 m near contact; a blocked step is bisected to keep the ship on the safe side. The entire remaining frame's travel is cancelled, including residual pulse speed, and throttle/navigation/pulse are cleared. A visible message explains how to climb or steer away. There is no damage or bounce simulation. Old stopped flight saves inside the new envelope may move toward greater clearance without a position snap.

Close manual flight requires the matching planet's dense contact core, with a 40 m edge margin and a front-hemisphere check. Outside that coverage, the conservative analytic 5 km guard remains. During delayed recentering, the ship stops at the dense coverage boundary instead of proceeding onto coarse triangles; throttle can resume after replacement arrives. The streaming planner now requests replacement after 550 m of movement, or earlier when the predicted focus reaches 800 m from the current center. The existing coverage guard remains the fallback for delayed workers. The visual capture `art/milestones/manual-low-flight.png` records the keyboard-controlled approach at about 20 m above ground.
